A day adds up in the line a recipe’s ingredient reads in — the same figures, the same separators, the same two glyphs, one span builder behind both. No g: the initial is against its number (90P), and the week’s thin thousands separator is the only thing it adds.

The tail is fibre, and it comes and goes. Fibre is optional per ingredient, so a set of meals states it only when every meal it counted did — Monday’s two both do; Tuesday and Thursday each carry a dish whose rows do not, so their lines stop at fat. The meal is still counted either way: an unstated fibre is not an exclusion, the denominator does not move, and nothing is named as needing fixing. It is a sheaf rather than a word, and energy is a flame, on the line where the two spelled-out units would be the longest things and the only two that never change; both carry the word they replaced to a screen reader, and both sit on the digits’ own cap-height centre. The whole line scales down together rather than dropping a digit (a truncated 1 234 is a wrong number, not a short one).

Nothing on this screen is new. The switcher, the lens, the day card, the cook marker and the add line are the ones above — the week simply begins on Sunday and ends on Saturday, and its title dates it from the Sunday. What that buys is the whole point: the shop you do on Sunday morning is the shop for the week whose first dinner is that night, so a Sunday meal is no longer stranded at the tail of the week that is ending. The cook marker follows it without being taught anything — the cook day is the earliest day the batch covers, so a batch cooked on shopping day now heads the week instead of trailing Monday’s.

The order is a household fact, set once in Account (D5), not a per-phone view: it decides which week a meal is stored in, so both phones must agree or they would write into two different weeks. Monday-start is unchanged and stays the default — this frame is the same screen under the other setting.

the head row and the column feet are pinned: the switcher, the lens and the seven day names stay put, and so does every day’s one ＋ add a meal and its total, while the matrix between them scrolls — no day can push either off screen. The seven columns run from the household’s first day, the day the week is addressed by, so a Sunday-start household reads Sunday first and Saturday last and the title dates the week from it. Rows are what the week has: Snack exists because Friday has two, and its other six cells are quiet paper — no box, no +, nothing to dismiss. A row is as tall as its own tallest day and no taller: Tuesday’s two lunches deepen the Lunch row and leave quiet paper either side of it, and no other row moves. And nothing here is a tile to be dragged: week v3 has no move, so the card is worked the way the phone row is — the title, the avatars, the −, all drawn.

a card prints the facts the phone row prints, and no others. A recipe the week has edited carries edited for this week under its cook marker on every card it appears on — Tuesday’s pot and Friday’s share of it — because one variant is held per week and recipe, and two cards of the same pot cannot disagree. The avatars and the portions chip are one target: they open the meal editor, a dialog at this width, whose fields are the slot, who is eating and the portions, with the edit for this week › door last. The title opens the recipe it names, carrying the week it is planned in. The foot carries the day’s energy and its denominator and nothing else — the rest of the line, and why a day refuses or leaves a meal out, is the next two frames’ question.
