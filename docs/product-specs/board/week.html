One line, in the denominator posture the macros above it already keep. To cook is the planned week at the latest prices, at the portions as planned; a recipe with an unpriced line has no cost at all, so its meal cannot join the figure and the lines to go and price are counted instead — an unpriced count where the macros carry a stub count. A snack is costed too — its own stated amount, carried to its row's basis the way its macros are, at that row's latest price — and joins the unpriced count only when the row has no price or nothing carries the amount to the basis; a meal eaten out is passed over, being neither a cost to cook nor a gap in one. The ≈ says a price is the latest one seen and not a quote, and the figure reads to the dollar because a sum over a week of them has no cents of precision (ADR-0017).

The second figure is what the week’s receipts came to, and it is drawn only when a receipt is dated inside the week on screen — a week nobody shopped for says nothing rather than $0 spent, which would read as a free week. It names where and when the money went (one store and its day, or the count of stores where there were several), and it is a door onto the receipts ledger. It appears on a week that plans nothing too: a shop is a fact about the paper, not about the plan. So the week is the left column now, a fixed 340, and the day is the page beside it.

every name, in the small voice. A day is a heading — its name at 15.5, its date, TODAY where it is, and what it holds at the right edge — then every meal as ONE run in the mono at 10.5, a faint dot between them, wrapping rather than truncating and wrapping rather than listing one per line. the pane a notch down, and in the same dialect. Day name 24, dish 17 — the plan slot's own size — and each meal's macros as the strip too, muted, so the screen speaks one macro grammar.
