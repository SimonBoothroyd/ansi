A matched line is one sentence with the money first. The amount slot the recipe card gives a quantity here gives the sum paid; the note under the name is the pack and what the two come to per basis. A line sold by weight needs no pack — the paper printed the pound and the rate, so the row reads them as they are and prices itself. A discount printed under an item is folded into that item’s sum, shown as a deduction on the same line, because what you paid is the price.

Two flags a recipe line never raises. Say what the pack is — a matched row that neither sells by weight nor has a pack yet: the door is the price sheet’s for field. The toggle beside it keeps the answer as a word on the row, which is a different gain: the pack carries over to the next receipt either way, and what a word buys is one the household can also say on a recipe line and the shop can say buy 3 of. Not food — paper towels, bag fees, a bottle deposit — moves the line under the fold, where it still counts toward the total and never toward a price. The vocabulary learns nothing, and the household remembers everything. A receipt’s words are a store’s abbreviations, so confirming one writes no alias and TJ ORG BANANAS never becomes a word the recipe door can see. What the next receipt inherits is the household’s own answers, both of them filed under the printed name: the pack — a line with no printed weight opens on the pack these words were last bought in for this same row, because one store’s words name one product and the 16 oz bag at one shop is not the 12 oz at another; words nobody has bought under before fall back to the pack the row was last bought in anywhere — and the match, recalled by the server per printed name off this household’s own saved receipt lines — exact, never fuzzy, and the most recently said answer wins, so correcting a saved receipt corrects the memory. A line that arrived that way says as you matched it last time beside its chosen row, because it is the one ✓ that can be wrong for a reason you can see — and changing it is the correction. Every open card has a PRICE chip, opening on the figure that was read: a $4.99 read as $4.49 is wrong without being zero, the join card can only say that something is, and this is where it is put right. An unmatched card is titled with the paper’s words for the thing, figures off — the money has its own column — and the verbatim line stays under it.

The week is the unit, because the shop is. A receipt files under the week its date falls in — the household’s own week start, so a Sunday shop sits inside the week it feeds. Each week closes with the pair the band shows, spent against planned, and a month line at the top sums the receipts by store. A receipt opens on the review it was confirmed on — one screen for a receipt, fresh or kept — so a wrong store, date, match, pack or figure is put right where it was first said; Save reads Save changes, stays shut until something has moved, rewrites the rows in place, and delete this receipt sits under it behind a question. Typed by hand is a receipt with no photo: the price sheet on an ingredient page writes a one-line receipt here, so a hand-typed price and a scanned one are the same fact in the same ledger.
