A row is bought in its own word. Lime’s lime, whole weighs what the row says a piece weighs, so every door says it and the shop counts it — 2½ lime, whole, the grams under the count and the round-up after them when the count is fractional (ADR-0016). A row that weighs a piece but names no size (Avocado) reads a count of pieces, ≈ when read back from a plain weight, and a piece line on any weighed row folds through that weight rather than sitting beside the grams unsummed. A row asked for in one named measure keeps that count (5 onion, medium)

What the trip costs sits on the sync line, where the one sentence about the whole list already lives, and each open row carries its own under the grams. A row's own figure wears ≈: a price is the latest one seen, not a quote. The trip figure reads to the dollar, because a sum over a dozen of them does not have cents of precision (ADR-0017) — and it wears ≈ only while the whole walk is priced. A row nothing can price adds nothing to the sum, so a walk holding one reads at least $58 still to buy · 1 row unpriced: the figure is a floor and says which it is, the way the week’s band does. A row with no price says so under its own grams rather than leaving a gap — but only once something on the trip HAS one, so a household that has priced nothing is never nagged — and that row is where the fix is made. The basket carries none: the line answers what is left to buy

The basket is one section, always at the bottom — under the aisles, above the echo rows — in aisle order then name, and keeps its aisles inside it a size down, so a ticked row is re-found where it was found. An aisle whose rows are all ticked leaves the top. A tap unticks, and the row returns to its aisle on the next derivation. Every row ticked: the aisles give way to one line, everything’s in the basket

The card says four words and no more. edited for this week, appended to the sub-line the card already prints. There is no note, no amber band, no second session — because nothing about the cook changed: one recipe, one week, one line set, one pot, one scale. The whole reason the override is keyed on (week, recipe) rather than on the meal is to keep this card exactly as it is

What it would have cost keyed per meal. A session is one pot, one scale, one line set; two covered meals that disagreed about what goes in could not share it. Tuesday and Saturday would split into two sessions, the freezer rescue would stop applying across the split (it merges distant instances of the same dish), and one swapped ingredient would silently cost a whole extra cook

the session builder is untouched: the plan still clusters by recipe, and the lines it expands are read once per recipe across all its sessions — which stays correct, because the variant is per week and recipe too

Two-up cards read as gappy and corporate: every card was mostly air around one small timeline, and the grid left a hole under a short one. The width’s real dividend is a shared axis — seven days drawn once, faint verticals carrying them through every row — so “what is cooking on Thursday” is a glance down a column instead of seven little bars compared by eye. A row with one session is short, and that is page, because no box is drawn round it.

Nothing about a session changes with the width: the same covers sentence, the same four-word edited for this week clause, the same split and freezer notes word for word, and a batch said in its own fraction. Every door the card had is on the row — the recipe name opens the recipe for this week, the whole-batch nudge is a line under the covers sentence whose tap the tick follows, and a component gap keeps its named reason and its Set the yield door. A gap’s day is a bare amber tick: the day is known, the scale is not, and none is invented.

The cost is a convention to learn — tick, dot, band, amber dot — so the sheet states it in a key once, under the axis. At 1180 the day pitch narrows and the name column gives up width; the seven-day axis and the centring never do.
