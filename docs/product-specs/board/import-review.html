The stages are real, they run in this order, and so are the times: import-recipe answers text/event-stream and emits an event as each stage completes, carrying its own elapsed. A finished row freezes at the server's number; the running row ticks. Nothing here is estimated — before the server's first event the screen claims no stages at all and shows only Sending….

What the width is spent on, and nothing else. The phone's review asks somebody to hold two things in their head at once: what the page said, and what we made of it. The only place the page survives there is the from source: line under each row. On a desk the page can simply be there — so the left column is the source at a readable measure and the row's duplicate from source: line goes, because the same words are now set larger, in order, five centimetres away. Under 1024 the line stays: it is the only copy there is.

A line is a row, not a card. The card's border was the affordance for expanding it, and expanding is what the panel took over — so the list is rows on bare paper, like the Library's ledger and the Shop's walk. Each keeps the phone's three parts (the mono amount in its own column, the identity, the note) and its grip, since drag was always a collapsed-row gesture and on wide every row is collapsed, which makes the whole list draggable rather than only the shut part of it. A flagged row has no amber box because it has no box at all: the amber is a 2 px rule in its margin, Cook's own device, carrying the same ⚠ tag and the same words. The selected row takes the Shop's herb wash — one idiom for "the pane is reading this one", in both views.

The bin, the optional switch, the amount door, the unit chips and the notes field are all in the panel, which is where they are on the phone: on its expanded card, never on the collapsed row. So nothing drawn was dropped and nothing moved between states — the state just stopped being a fold and became a column. The panel's head says which line it holds and where that line sits (3 of 8), the header form stays at the top of the lines column where the phone has it, and the method's step cards go on down the same column under the ingredients.

The commit bar is the lines column's footer, not the page's. The number on it is about the lines, and a bar drawn across the source pane would say the page has something to save. It reads the same map the header count and every row flag read, which is the one rule this screen has always had.

At 1180 the source gives up its width first, to a 300 floor, and the panel after it; the lines never go under 440, because they are what a person is reading. Below 1024 this frame does not exist — the phone review, expanding in place, is unchanged.

Two things this frame rests on. the state keeps the request — ImportReconciling and ImportLoading carry the ImportSource, which is what the left column draws from (a photo import's pages are files the payload never mentions) — and the payload carries two additive fields: source_text, the fetched page's visible text bounded at 20k characters, and a per-line source_span into that exact string. Both are omitted when there is nothing to say, so a photo import's payload is byte-identical to the one before them. The span is located, not guessed: the server looks for the line's own verbatim printed words in the text it is about to send and emits a range only where they can be pointed at unambiguously — so a link whose page could not be placed lights nothing and this column shows the plain page, which is the honest fallback. No box is drawn on a photo, then or ever: nothing on the wire carries one.

One thing the code adds to what is drawn: the panel’s head carries the card’s own chevron beside its bin, because the panel is the phone’s expanded card and that is the control that shuts it — here it hands the panel back to the work queue. Everything else on it is the card’s, in the card’s order.

An empty panel beside a list of work would be the width wasted. With nothing selected the panel says what is left, grouped by what it wants rather than by line order — which is the one thing a twenty-line import is hard to see: that four of the five outstanding lines are the same job done four times. It is a view, not a feature: no new number, no new state, nothing that can disagree with a row. The groups are the ⚠ labels the rows already carry, in the order the phone's gate checks them.

The piece-weight item is not a new flag. A counted line on a row with no weight meets the ordinary unsupported-unit gate, which is why it sits under Pick a supported unit with the cilantro's bunch — and why its one door is the row, since what one of a thing weighs is the ingredient's property and never the import's. Created here is drawn apart and quiet for the opposite reason: a stub commits perfectly well, so it is out of the count, and saying so is the whole point of putting it on screen.

The queue does not replace the rows. Every flag is still on its own line, with its own words, where somebody scrolling will meet it — the phone's screen is the one that has to work alone, and nothing here is only reachable from the panel.
