Three beats · what moves and what refuses to — a tab switch is directionless, a push is directional, and the edge-swipe tracks the finger

Content cross-fades in place. Nothing translates, so Library→Shop and Shop→Library are the same animation — there is no “wrong direction” left to have. The bar does not move, fade or rebuild; only the selected item ticks from muted to herb.

A recipe, the editor, import and the ingredients manager cover the bar — they are pushed above the whole shell, not inside a tab. Here the slide is correct and wanted: it comes in from the side the back gesture pulls it out from. Cupertino on iOS, predictive-back on Android — each platform’s own.

one dock, drawn by the shell, on every route: above the bar on a tab, at the screen’s foot on a pushed page (the bar is covered there, the dock is not). It is absent while nothing runs — no empty state, no timers tab

collapsed it is one row, not a strip of pills: a recipe’s title is the only thing that tells two timers apart, and titles here run to thirty and forty characters. A row gives the title the whole width — the step in the mono column the method uses, the title cut at its end with an ellipsis, the count. A title is never shortened to a word: Slow-Cooker and Sticky name nothing

the row is the one that needs you first: a due timer before all, then the soonest. +2 says how many more and opens the list. The row wears the chip’s four states — draining herb, dashed when paused, Gone red and counting on once due

a tap on the row pushes the recipe at its step — /recipes/:id?step=4 opens on Method, scrolled to it, the chip flashing once. From that recipe’s own page it only scrolls

every timer is held by its end time, kept on this phone and nowhere else: a slept or killed app reads the right number when it wakes, and the other cook’s phone never sees it

a ring on time needs Android’s exact alarm permission, and Play allows that only to an app whose timers are a real feature — which these are. Notification permission is asked on the first timer started, never at launch

starting a timer on the web turns Keep screen on on by itself (the Screen Wake Lock), because a browser whose screen has slept is a silent one. The sun says so, in the bar and on the dock, and the browser drops the lock when the tab is hidden — it is taken again when the tab comes back

the tab’s title counts the soonest timer down — ⏱ 3:10 · Ansi — so at a desk with ten tabs it is still in sight. If the browser grants it, a hidden tab shows a notification at the end time; a throttled tab may show it up to a minute late, and the end time keeps the number right regardless

the line under the dock is said once, the first time a timer starts in this browser, and not again. It is the web’s whole limit in one sentence: there is no push service behind a kitchen timer
