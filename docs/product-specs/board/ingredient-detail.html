Advice, never a refusal. The dock owns the refusals; this is the derivation’s own voice, saying what the app noticed about the figures on screen while the pack is still in somebody’s hand. Save stays live, and a panel read off a label is the household’s whatever the arithmetic makes of it.

Two rules, both deliberately loose. Protein, fat and carb all zero on a food that states calories — energy comes from somewhere. And a fibre-adjusted Atwater gap: 4P + 9F + 4C, with a stated fibre moved off the 4 and onto a 2, has to miss the stated energy by more than both 150 kcal and half of it. The flat floor is what keeps a vinegar (18 kcal, almost none of it Atwater) and a glass of red wine quiet; the proportion is what stops the floor swallowing a real mistake on a dense food. Baking powder and a high-fibre cocoa pass on the same slack.

An empty fibre field costs the row nothing. The dock is live and the row completes on the four — most labels outside the US print no fibre at all, and a row that waited on one would never be finished. Typing one stores it beside the four; leaving it blank stores nothing, never a zero (invariant 3), and a stated 0 is a reading that means something different.

Fibre alone is refused. A number in this field with the four blank says Fibre is an extra figure on a panel, not a panel of its own — give it a number beside the four, or clear it. It qualifies a panel; it is not one.
