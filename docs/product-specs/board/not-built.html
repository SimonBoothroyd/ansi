A matched line is one sentence with the money first. The amount slot the recipe card gives a quantity here gives the sum paid; the note under the name is the pack and what the two come to per basis. A line sold by weight needs no pack — the paper printed the pound and the rate, so the row reads them as they are and prices itself. A discount printed under an item is folded into that item’s sum, shown as a deduction on the same line, because what you paid is the price.

Two flags a recipe line never raises. Say what the pack is — a matched row that neither sells by weight nor has a pack yet: the door is the price sheet’s for field, and the toggle keeps the answer as a measure on the row so it is asked once. Not food — paper towels, bag fees, a bottle deposit — moves the line under the fold, where it still counts toward the total and never toward a price. The match is the server’s, each time. A receipt’s words are a store’s abbreviations, and confirming one teaches the vocabulary nothing; the next receipt is matched afresh. What carries over is the pack, on the row.
